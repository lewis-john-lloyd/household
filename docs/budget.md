# Categories, budget and the monthly checklist

How transactions are categorized and reviewed, budget lines, and the monthly reports.

## Categories and the review tab

Every transaction gets a category and a record of what decided it (`categorize.py`), first match wins: a category a person set on the line; a payee rule (`payee_rules`, a pattern with an effective date, seeded from the inbox like the accounts); an answer given in review for that payee; an explanation matched to the line (see the Inbox section; an Amazon match decides the category, a PayPal match names the payee and categorization carries on), or one still to come (Amazon, PayPal, Venmo, Apple and Google lines after their latest export wait, with a provisional guess); a scikit-learn model retrained every run on the old books and every answer since, which decides only when it is confident; and the bank's own category, as a guess.

A transfer with no partner is asked about every time (money out is guessed as the household parameter `unpaired_transfer_guess`), except one dated before the other account's downloaded history begins (banks offer different lengths of history): that one is taken to be a transfer with that account, the one a household parameter `transfer_payee:<account>` names by payee or else the main account in `transfer_account`, and `transfers` lists it as `assumed_before_history`, and paper checks are asked about one by one. Lines before `REVIEW_FROM` are never asked about.

Everything else lands in the ledger's `review` tab, one row per merchant (`key`, the payee with store numbers, places and processor prefixes stripped) or per line. Type a category in `answer`, or `y` to take the guess, and run ingest: the answers are stored (`proposal_decisions` for a merchant, `txn_corrections` for a line) and the tab is rewritten with what is still open. A merchant answer covers every line from that merchant, past and future, however a card spells it: spaces and apostrophes don't count, a key covers every key that starts with it, and names a card cut short match on their first ten letters (`categorize.same_merchant`); `example` shows up to three spellings that were joined.

Shorten the key before answering to widen it (`AMAZON MKTPL` to `AMAZON`). Old category names in `category_aliases` apply to rules and answers too, so renaming a category is one alias row. To split a line among categories, type the parts in its `split` column (the `answer` column has a dropdown), `kids 20; hobby 15.50; gifts 40; family`: a category and an amount for each, with at most one part left without an amount to take the rest. A sign is ignored: every part takes the line's own, so a charge's parts are charges and a refund's are refunds. The parts must come to the line's amount to the cent; a split that doesn't stays on its row with the reason.

To split a line that is already decided (a rule or a merchant answer covers it), add a row to the review tab by hand with its `txn_id` (from Reports `categorized`) as `key`, `line` as `kind` and the parts in `split`. A split is stored as a `txn_corrections` row with field `split`, and the line's category and its split replace each other, so whichever was recorded later stands; budget against actual, the Sankey and the transactions list count each part in its own category. `engine.review` turns answers around without touching the inbox. The views answer the same questions without the tab (see [Editing in the views](views.md#editing-in-the-views)).

## Budget

`budget_rules` holds one row per budget line per change: the category it pays for, the account that funds it, a priority, and a rule (`budget.py`). `fixed` is an amount per period (monthly, biweekly, annually and so on) normalized to a month; `seasonal` is the mean plus a quarter standard deviation of the category's spending in the three months centered on the same month a year earlier, the old budget-reports formula, with the amount as a fallback until there is a year of history; `trailing` is the same formula over the four months before the budget month, for spending that follows recent habits more than the season; `lookback` is the same formula over the twelve months before, a year-round average; both fall back to the amount until there is history; `sinking_fund` sets an amount aside each month and keeps a balance against the category's spending; `recurring` is the recurring charges the line pays (see below), else the ones you track in its category that pay no other line, each at its latest price on a monthly footing (a yearly one is a twelfth a month), plus the amount as an allowance for the rest of its spending, so a price change moves the target with it (`recurring.line_expected`, `recurring.monthly_expected`); `loan` is the line's category's part of that month's scheduled loan payment (interest, or principal with any extra), from the amortization schedule and the loan's `loan_split` setting; `scheduled` puts one amount in one month (several rows for installments); `end` stops a line.

A loan's payment is one charge, but its parts budget and change apart. With `loan_split:<loan id>` set (`mortgage_interest=interest, mortgage_principal=principal, pmi=50, property_tax=200, home_insurance=50`, say), each month's payment line splits into those categories (`mortgage.split_payments`, as the books are categorized): interest and principal as the schedule has them that month, and what the payment leaves after them, the escrow, shared across the escrow parts by their amounts, so the parts add to the payment and an escrow review that moves the payment shows in each of them. A month is split only when one loan with the setting is running and the payment covers its principal and interest; the split payment still counts toward the loan beside the schedule. Its interest and principal lines take the `loan` rule; the escrow parts take `fixed` amounts, and a loan line's drawer opens the schedule on the Home page.

Giving is budgeted like any other group: a giving line's amount is the year's estimate. Under each line item of the `giving` group the Budget page lists the period's gifts by recipient (`giving.py`, the `giving` table): a recipient is the gift's merchant key, shown by the name given it in the Recurring editor or else the payee text seen most, with what it was given and how many gifts. Each recipient is marked deductible or not right there, a `recipient` edit (kept in `proposal_decisions` as `deductible:<key>`, approve for yes); a row at the foot of the group gives the year's gifts to recipients marked deductible, and how many of the year's recipients are not marked yet, for the itemized deduction.

A change is a new row with a later `effective`, so past targets never move. Compute writes `budget_targets` (each line each month), `budget_vs_actual` (each category each month, with its group and priority), `sinking_funds`, and `daily_actuals` (every day's spending per category, every era, transfers and income left out) for the Budget page's comparisons. The rules arrive as a seed file and are changed afterwards from the views (see [Editing in the views](views.md#editing-in-the-views)): a change is a new row, and of two rows in one month the later one is in force, so a correction never edits a row. A later `scheduled` row on the same date replaces that amount (0 cancels it).

## The monthly checklist

Compute writes four reports for the monthly routine (`checks.py`, `recurring.py`). `coverage` lists each bank and card account that sends monthly statements with the newest date the ledger has for it, and marks it due after `max_gap_days` (45 when blank); then each explanation download with the last date it covers and how many lines wait for the next one. Set an account's `expected_docs` to `when_used` for an open card kept unused (listed, never due), or to `none` (or fill `closed`) to take it off the list. `balance_check` turns each stored balance into the balance the ledger implies before its first transaction; every snapshot of an account must imply the same one, so a later download that disagrees points at missing or doubled transactions.

`paychecks` pairs each stub's net pay with its pay deposit (same amount, a few days around payday, the deposit naming the employer) and lists what doesn't pair: a deposit with no stub is "missing stub: did you forget it?", and a stub whose pay never arrived is "no deposit". `recurring` lists payees charged at a steady cadence for a steady amount, marking the ones that changed price or stopped. Those are suggestions: the Budget page's Recurring tab tracks a merchant (review's name for it, such as APPLE ONE) as a recurring charge or not, whether it is a mortgage, a lease, insurance or a streaming service (a `subscription` edit, kept in `proposal_decisions`; the internal names keep the word so marks already saved still count), offers each charge that recurs and is not tracked either way (track it, merge it into one that is, or turn it down; the ones that seem to have stopped, with no charge for 1.5 cadences, are listed apart), and opens any charge for its forms.

A charge says the budget line it pays: a `recurring_line` edit (kept in `proposal_decisions` as `subscription_line:<key>`, the line in its note; a blank line unlinks it), picked on the line's form among its charges or on the charge itself, which tracks the charge and, on the charge, makes the line `recurring`. So a line that is one charge (the phone, the internet, one streaming service) follows that charge even when other lines share its category. Compute warns when a linked line is gone or isn't `recurring`. Two entries that are one charge (a new name on the statement, a loan that was sold) are merged there: a `recurring_merge` edit (kept in `proposal_decisions` as `subscription_merge:<key>`, the key it joins in its note) adds one key's charges to another's, so they have one history, cadence and price change; an empty `same_as` separates them again (`recurring.merges`). The tab lists what you track by the budget line it belongs to (`recurring.subscriptions`, the `subscriptions` table): each with its cadence, latest charge, next charge expected and yearly cost (the latest charge times the charges a year, or the last 365 days when irregular), and the yearly total of the ones still charged.

When a charge differs from the one before by more than 3% (and 50 cents), the tab shows the change and what it means for its line (a `recurring` line follows it; a fixed one is offered the new amount), and for 45 days after the charge a status card and the weekly email say so. On the Budget page, each tracked charge is its own entry under its category's line, with what it spent and what its cadence expected (`subscription_months`: the last charge for a monthly one, and for a yearly or quarterly one the last charge in the month it fell due); its spending is part of the line above. A group of two or more line items (categories) has a row of its own with their totals and how many it adds up; a group of one planned line item is just that row, its group's name a tag on it. A category paid by two or more budget lines shows how many on its own row, and each line as its own row under it, with its rule in words and its part of the period's target (`budget_targets`); clicking one opens a drawer with only that line to change. Spending stays the category's, so a line's row has no spent of its own.
