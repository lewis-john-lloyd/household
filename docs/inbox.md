# The inbox

How files dropped in the Drive inbox become ledger rows. Investment files are in [investments.md](investments.md).

## The inbox

Files dropped in the Drive `inbox` folder are identified, checked and filed by `engine.ingest_inbox`: pay stubs in three layouts (one of them a payroll provider's earnings statement, whose PDF is read piece by piece because its layout text is unusable), a brokerage's equity award export, the accounts seed, tax returns and W-4s (filed for the record, no rows), and the IRS publications, revenue procedures, notices and public laws the tax code cites (filed under stable names such as `publication-15-t-2026`, no rows). Change sets saved from the views are read here too (see [Editing in the views](views.md#editing-in-the-views)), oldest first. Every parser checks the document against its own printed totals; a file that fails stays in the inbox with the reason, and nothing is written.

Re-sent files, and re-downloads whose rows are all already present, go to `record_store/duplicates`, except a file that was filed unread (a pay stub in a layout no parser knew yet), which is read again when it is sent again; a partially overlapping export adds only its new rows and names the earlier file it overlapped. Compute lists likely double entries (same account and amount, a few days apart, from different files) in the `possible_duplicates` report; a `void` correction takes a confirmed one off the books. See `src/household/inbox.py` and `compute.possible_duplicates`.

Bank and card downloads come in as OFX or QFX (preferred: each transaction carries the bank's own id, and the file gives the balance) or as two banks' own CSV layouts (one of them one statement per file, named with the card's last four). An OFX file is matched to its account by the last four of the account number against the `last4` column of `accounts`; a CSV names no account, so its file name must contain the `account_id` (`checking_bank.csv`). Transactions get stable ids (the bank's FITID, or account, date, amount and payee with a count for identical rows), so overlapping downloads add each transaction once. Pending rows are left out, card CSV amounts are flipped so money out is negative everywhere, and an OFX ledger balance goes to `balances`. See `parsers/ofx.py`, the CSV parsers and `ingest.finish_bank_rows`. PayPal's QIF downloads are not counted as an account (each purchase is already on the card or bank that funded it); their payments, with counterparty and item, go to `payment_details` for naming those lines.

Amazon's order history export (the zip from Request your data, or its `Order History.csv` and `Refund Details.csv` on their own) goes to `order_items`: order, ship date, the item's total, department, website and the card's last four, nothing else. The inbox files that trimmed copy in `record_store` and moves the original to Drive's trash, since the export also holds addresses, tracking numbers and the like; the same export sent again goes straight to the trash. See `parsers/amazon.py`.

Google Takeout (Google Pay and Google Play Store selected; it may arrive as several zips) goes to `order_items` too: Google Pay's list of Google purchases (Play apps and subscriptions, Google One, the Google Store), service `google`, app and item, amount and card's last four. The zips go to the trash and only those rows are filed; a part holding nothing read (the HTML index) is trashed as well. See `parsers/google_takeout.py`.

`explain.py` matches these explanation downloads to the lines they explain, by amount and date, one to one: a PAYPAL line takes PayPal's counterparty as its payee, and a GOOGLE line the app and item bought; an AMAZON charge matches a shipment (or a whole order, or one item of a shipment for a store that charges each part on its own), a credit a refund, and takes its category from the items (Whole Foods is food, a few departments map, the rest is household), split by item amount when they differ. Any store whose order history lists items works the same way: a parser that writes its orders as `order_items` rows and an entry in `explain.ORDER_SERVICES` (the store's name and the pattern of its card lines) is all it takes, and its items then show in the views as Amazon's do.

An order history decides a line ahead of any payee rule, so a store asked about line by line until its export arrives is itemized once it does, and a rule for the store still decides the lines its history doesn't explain. A store with no export can still come in as a purchase list read from its account in a browser (see `parsers/target.py` for the columns): each item's amount is its share of the order total, tax and discounts included, and clothes and shoes count as clothing. Orders paid with a card that is not in `accounts` are left out. A line inside an export's dates that matched nothing is categorized like any other, except a line from a store with an order history and no payee rule (AMAZON): that one is asked about on its own in review, with the items shipped around then (closest in price first) as hints, since a gift card or a split payment can hide the order it paid for.

A line after the latest export waits for the next; a line before the earliest goes to review, since nothing will ever cover it. A Venmo cash-out is always asked about on its own, since each carries whatever came in since the last. A PayPal, Venmo, Apple or Google line nothing names is asked about on its own, not as one merchant, since its payee names only the service (an answer already given for the merchant still covers it).
