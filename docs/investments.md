# Investments

Investment downloads and statements, holdings and prices, filling gaps between readings, and tax lots.

## Downloads and statements

Fidelity's account history (Accounts, History, Download: `History_for_Account_<number>.csv`, for a brokerage account, an IRA or an HSA) and a workplace plan's history in Quicken's investment format (`.qif`) go to `investment_activity`, one row per thing that happened, in one vocabulary for both: its kind (contribution, buy, sell, exchange, reinvest, dividend, interest, conversion in or out, transfer in or out, withdrawal, fee, adjustment), the change in shares of its fund and their value, and the money crossing the account's edge (in positive, out negative; buying, selling and exchanging inside the account move none). A history file finds its account by the last four of the number in its name, against the accounts table's `last4`; a plan's QIF names no account, so its file name must carry the account_id (`m_401k_old.qif`). Overlapping downloads add each row once (a history row is keyed with its running cash balance, a QIF row on all its fields). Compute sums each account's money in and out by year and kind into `investment_flows`, and logs the contributions, conversions and withdrawals. See `parsers/fidelity.py`.

An OFX or QFX investment download (Vanguard's puts every account in one file) is read by `parsers/ofx_invest.py` into the same `investment_activity`, plus each account's positions as `holdings` and every price the file states as `prices` (the only prices a plan-only trust fund has). Each statement finds its account by the last four of its number in the accounts table's `last4`, so one file can fill several accounts. A workplace plan's rows keep their money source (`source`: pretax, match, aftertax, rollover, as the custodian names it); a plan is kept as one account and its parts are told apart by source. In a plan, a buy is payroll money coming in (a contribution) and a transfer moves shares between funds or sources inside it. Elsewhere, a deposit that shows up only as a sweep into the settlement fund is read as a contribution, worked out from the cash the account had on hand, and a transfer out of a pre-tax account matched by one into a Roth account on the same day is a conversion. A transaction the file lists twice, identical to its FITID, is read once (Vanguard repeats every trust-fund transaction).

HealthEquity's "All Investment Transactions" download (`.xlsx`) is read by `parsers/healthequity.py`: a Buy moves the HSA's cash into its fund (no money in or out), a Dividend is reinvested, each row's running Total Shares is checked against the row before, and each fund's last total is a `holdings` reading. The file names no account, so its name carries the account_id (`l_hsa_healthequity.xlsx`). The inbox reads any `.xlsx` as CSV text of its first sheet (`xlsx.py`, standard library only).

Schwab's Equity Award Center transaction export, CSV or JSON, is read by `parsers/schwab_equity.py` into `equity_vests` and `equity_sales` (each sale's lots must add up to its shares and proceeds). Equity grants' vesting schedules come from Schwab's Awards page: copy each grant's details (award, Award ID with vested, unvested and canceled, and the Vesting Schedule) as text into a `.txt` and drop it in the inbox. `parsers/schwab_awards.py` checks that each grant's schedule and its vested, unvested and canceled shares add up to the shares granted, and writes `vest_schedule` (grant, award date, symbol, type, vest date, shares); a grant's rows from its latest copy are its schedule. The tax projection then takes the vests left this year from the schedule at the 12-month average share price (the last price of each month in the year, from vests' fair market values and closes in `prices`), in place of one a month at the recent average.

A Vanguard workplace plan's quarterly statement (PDF) is read by `parsers/vanguard_plan.py` for what the plan's download lacks: the balance in each money source at the quarter's end (pre-tax, match, rollover, after-tax, each year's Roth in-plan conversion), each a `holdings` reading with no fund and the source's name as `source`. Every source and fund row must add up, the sources to the Total Value and the funds to the Total Invested, or the statement is refused. The plan's number in the header finds its account by `last4`.

A Fidelity NetBenefits plan statement (Statement Details for any period, saved as a PDF) is read by `parsers/netbenefits_statement.py`: each fund's shares and value as of the day before the period and at its end, each a `holdings` reading with the fund's name as printed as its symbol (a name wrapped onto the next line joined back). Shares times price must be the value and the funds must add up to the Account Totals at both dates, or the statement is refused. The header names the plan but not its number, so the file's name finds the account (`m_plan_q1_2025.pdf`). A run of quarterly statements gives a reading every quarter, which fills an account's history.

Between readings, a value carried as read moves with the market (`valuation.fill_gaps`): a fund with closes of its own, or of the public fund the `price_proxy:<symbol>` setting names (the symbol lower case, other characters as `_`: `price_proxy:s_p_500_index` set to an index fund's ticker), follows them from its reading (basis `tracked`); and when a later reading of the account exists, the values also close the remaining gap a little each month (`bridged`), so the history climbs to the next reading instead of sitting flat and jumping. A next reading more than twice or under half the market's path stays a jump (money moved in or out at once). An account whose value today rests on a reading more than six months old is warned about in the run log.

An account can be renamed with the `account_renamed:<id>` setting: the ledger keeps the id its rows were written with, compute and the views read every account column under the new name, and an inbox file named with either name finds the account.

A download that names a row wrong (a plan's QIF that calls money rolled in from an IRA a contribution) is corrected without changing the row: an `activity_corrections` seed (`activity_id,field,value,recorded_at,reason,source`, the activity_id from the ledger's `investment_activity`) gives the row's `kind` as it should be read, and compute reads it so. A rollover read as `transfer_in` is no longer counted as a contribution in the flows or the retirement plan.

When the old investments workbook's tab for one account was read into another (an old rollover IRA read into the IRA that took its place), the `legacy_account:<id>` setting names the account those readings belong to: compute reads the workbook's readings under `<id>` as that account's, and every other reading stays with `<id>` (or its new name). Both must be accounts in the ledger; give the old one its `closed` date so it holds nothing after. The legacy import notes a row dated before the row above it, which is usually a year typed wrong.

Edvest's 529 account download (`ofxdownload_MMDDYYYY.csv`: positions, then transactions) is read by `parsers/edvest.py`: contributions are money in, a rollover from another plan is a transfer in, a K-12 or college withdrawal is money out, and the positions are `holdings` dated from the download's name (keep it). A portfolio has no ticker, so its name is its symbol. The account is found by the last four digits of the account number.

HealthEquity's HSA cash history (the yearly "Completed Transactions" download, `TransactionHistory.xls`, an HTML table) is read by `parsers/healthequity_cash.py`: contributions and interest are money in, fees and claims paid money out, and an Investment row is the cash moving into a fund (inside the account). Read oldest first, each row's balance must follow from the one before; the newest balance is a `cash` holdings reading. The file names no account, so it is matched to the one HealthEquity account in the accounts table, or by an account_id in its name.

Fidelity's Positions download (`Portfolio_Positions_<date>.csv`) is read by `parsers/fidelity_positions.py`: every account's holdings on the download date, a money market held as cash at a share a dollar, and a plan's fund by its code (NON40OJU1 as OJU1, as the plan's QIF names it); each account by the last four digits of its number.

A stock option grant's schedule, copied as text from the plan's Stock Options page into a `.txt`, is read by `parsers/stock_options.py` into the same `vest_schedule` (award type OPTION, with its grant price and expiry); the tranches must add up to the grant. An option vesting is no income, so the tax projection takes only share awards (RSU, PSU) from the schedule.

Holdings and prices sit beside the activity. The ledger's `securities` tab lists each fund or stock with its name, asset class, expense ratio and `quote`, the symbol GOOGLEFINANCE knows it by (blank for a fund with no public price, such as a plan-only trust). `holdings` keeps readings of what an account held on a date: shares and value per fund, or a value alone. The legacy investments workbook comes in through `engine.ingest_legacy_investments(session, folder_id, workbook_id, tabs)`, which the engine notebook runs every time (with nothing new it writes nothing), where `tabs` maps each account tab to its account_id (`{"alex-ira": "a_ira"}`; several tabs can map to one account, and their readings on a date add up): its funds tab becomes `securities`, each fund quoted by its own symbol, and each tab's dated rows become `holdings` (`legacy_investments.py`).

A target-date fund kept as its share of each underlying fund comes in as those values, with no shares. Running it again adds nothing. Prices come from GOOGLEFINANCE through Sheets, so the engine never reaches the network itself: each ingest writes one formula per quoted security and date still wanted into the ledger's `price_requests` tab, waits up to a minute for Sheets to work them out, reads the answers into `prices` (the close on or before the date, the trading day it came from, and the source), and rewrites the tab with what is left for the next ingest, at most 600 at a time. The formula is built from the quote and date alone, each checked first, and `price_requests` is the one tab a formula is ever written to.

Prices are wanted on every date the legacy workbook recorded, then at each month-end after the last of those (from the first holdings or activity when there is no legacy workbook), and each security's latest close, asked each day ingest runs, so the 12-month average the tax projection prices vests at runs to the market's latest close. `engine.fetch_prices` asks round after round until none is wanted, for a backfill in one call. A date GOOGLEFINANCE has no price for is kept as a row with no close, so it is not asked again; delete the row to ask again. A fund's close is its NAV, unadjusted, which is what its shares are worth. See `prices.py`.

Tax lots and wash sales are worked out every compute run (`lots.py`). Each vest's net shares (`equity_vests`, at the vest's market value) and each purchase or reinvested dividend (`investment_activity`) is a lot; a stock plan sale names its lot by grant and vest date, and any other sale takes the account's oldest shares first. Splits come from the ledger's `stock_splits` tab, a seed file (`symbol,date,ratio,note`, one share becoming `ratio` shares from `date` on), and every share count is restated in today's shares. A loss is a wash for the shares of the same symbol, or the same `securities.wash_group`, acquired within 30 days either side, in any account: other lots, earliest first, each share replacing once.

The disallowed loss moves into the replacement shares' basis and holding period; in an IRA, a 401(k), an HSA or a 529 it is lost for good. Compute writes `form_8949` (each lot part sold, with code W and the adjustment for a wash) and `open_lots`, notes each year's gains beside the filed return's, and the tax projection counts the adjusted gains. Years before the plan's own records come from an old workbook's grants and sales tabs through `engine.ingest_legacy_equity(session, folder_id, workbook_id, grant_columns, grants_tab, sales_tab)`, where `grant_columns` maps each grant column of the grants tab to its grant id (`{"2001": "G1"}`); it adds only what is dated before the plan's records and is safe to run every time (`legacy_equity.py`).
